A home cage where mice learn to head-fix themselves

Many mouse experiments need the animal's head held still. I designed a home cage where mice train on their own and learn to place their head in a holder, which then fixes it in place automatically for two-photon imaging.

The problem

What made it hard

Most mouse neuroscience experiments require head fixation. A well-trained person can head-fix a mouse by hand, but the process makes the mouse anxious and keeps a person involved at every step.

Manual training also sets the schedule. Someone has to put each mouse in, test the sensors and run every session, which limits how efficiently training can happen.

My work

What I built

  • Training at home

    Mice train in their own cage on a systematic schedule, which reduces the human effect on training.

  • Voluntary head restraint

    Each mouse learns to place its head plate into the holder by itself.

  • Automatic fixation

    During an experiment the holder fixes the head automatically, ready for two-photon imaging.
