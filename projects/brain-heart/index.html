Brain-heart connectivity with multimodal deep learning

When the heart stops, brain activity goes quiet within a minute, and survivors of cardiac arrest can be left with lasting brain damage. Using data from a cohort of more than 1,000 animals, I combined EEG, ECG, other time series and tabular data to predict outcomes, and used interpretable models to see which signals drove each prediction.

The problem

What made it hard

Understanding how the heart and the brain affect each other during cardiac arrest could help prevent permanent brain damage. We found that the onset of a specific cardiac arrhythmia during the arrest might be associated with brain damage after resuscitation.

Each animal comes with several kinds of data: EEG, ECG, other time series and tabular records. The model has to fuse them into one prediction, and it has to show which signals drove that prediction.

My work

What I built

  • Hybrid CNN-LSTM

    Convolutional layers pick up local patterns in the signals and an LSTM follows how they change over time (+3% AUC).

  • Classical models

    Tuned random forest, XGBoost and LightGBM models.

  • Interpretability

    Used SHAP to show which features drove each prediction.

  • Impact

    The results contributed to a funded NIH R01 grant.

  • Linking heart and brain

    Applied several statistical tests across several models to link the onset of the arrhythmia with brain damage after resuscitation.
