My PhD dissertation · Boston University · 2026

Mapping a memory circuit, synapse by synapse

Can the part of the brain that feels touch also help hold a memory? I recorded neurons in a mouse's touch cortex while it remembered and compared touches, then mapped the wiring between those same neurons with electron microscopy.

Structural and Functional Organization of Somatosensory Cortex Microcircuits in Working-Memory-Related Processing. Advisor: Jerry L. Chen.

Background

What is connectomics?

A connectome is a wiring diagram of a brain: every neuron and every connection between them. The thinnest wires, axons, can be about 100 nanometers across, far too small to follow with an ordinary light microscope.

So the tissue is cut into sections around 50 nanometers thick, every section is imaged with an electron microscope, and each neuron is traced through the stack, first by AI and then by people who fix its mistakes.

MICrONS, 2025

One cubic millimeter of mouse visual cortex, about a grain of sand: roughly 200,000 cells, half a billion synapses and 1.6 petabytes of images.

My volume

Part of the mouse touch cortex (S1 and S2): 2,323 slices of 50 nm stacked about 116 µm deep, imaged at 3.75 nm per pixel. 657 TB of raw images.

Sources: White et al. 1986 · Winding et al., Science 2023 · Dorkenwald et al., Nature 2024 · Herculano-Houzel et al. 2006 · Azevedo et al. 2009 · MICrONS Consortium, Nature 2025.
