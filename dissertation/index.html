My PhD

I built one of these for my PhD

My dataset is the same kind as MICrONS, on a smaller scale: activity first, then wiring, in the same neurons. A mouse learned to compare two touches separated by a delay, and I recorded neurons in its touch cortex with two-photon imaging while it worked.

Then the same tissue went under the electron microscope: 2,323 slices of 50 nm, about 120 µm deep, imaged at 3.75 nm per pixel, for 657 TB of raw images. I ran the data operations for the whole volume and led the 15-person team that traced and proofread its neurons.

Background

What is connectomics?

A connectome is a wiring diagram of a brain: every neuron and every connection between them. The thinnest wires, axons, can be about 100 nanometers across, far too small to follow with an ordinary light microscope.

So the tissue is cut into sections around 50 nanometers thick, every section is imaged with an electron microscope, and each neuron is traced through the stack, first by AI and then by people who fix its mistakes.

Sources: White et al. 1986 · Winding et al., Science 2023 · Dorkenwald et al., Nature 2024 · Herculano-Houzel et al. 2006 · Azevedo et al. 2009.

The engineering

Building the map, then learning from it

Alongside the science, the connectomics work ran in two stages: building the dataset, then teaching models to learn from it.

Stage 1 · Build the dataset

Data operations for a 657 TB EM volume

Harvard Medical School, Visiting PhD Student · 2021 to 2025

  • Ran data operations across acquisition, preprocessing, GCP migration and segmentation handoff to an external vendor, then built the downstream ML analysis on the returned data.
  • Extended 2D stitching and 3D reconstruction on a Slurm HPC cluster for a 7× speedup and 85% lower memory.
  • Led a 15-person team (undergraduates and a postdoc) with no formal authority to deliver 415 proofread neuron reconstructions in about 1.5 years.
Stage 2 · Learn from it

Self-supervised contrastive learning for connectomics

Boston University, Graduate Research Assistant · 2024 to 2026

  • Reimplemented SegCLR and SynapseCLR from TensorFlow in PyTorch, with a custom normalization method to bridge data domains, and refined SynapseCLR on synapses from this volume.
  • Scaled contrastive pretraining across 2 V100 GPUs with PyTorch DistributedDataParallel, learning view-invariant embeddings of neurons.
  • Trained on about 1 TB of 3D EM with out-of-core loading of pre-extracted 256 × 256 × 256 cutouts.
  • Shipped a reproducible package for downstream classifiers (best model 0.86 accuracy), so cell-type labels can reach beyond the hand-annotated neurons.
